# CLDN4 trafficking — catalog notes

Numbers below are copied from retrieved text. Tags: `full_text`, `abstract_only`.

## Stimulus-induced CLDN4 internalization (primary)

### Cong et al., 2015 — mAChR / ERK / β-arrestin2 / clathrin

- **PMID** 25948584 | **DOI** 10.1242/jcs.165878 | *J Cell Sci* 128:2271–2286
- **Access:** `full_text` (publisher HTML)
- System: rat salivary SMG-C6 monolayers.
- Basal TER of untreated monolayers: 632.8±107.1 Ω·cm².
- Carbachol: 0.01 μmol/l TER drop significant from 20 min; 0.1 μmol/l from 10 to 60 min; 1 and 10 μmol/l from 5 min. Subsequent work used 1 μmol/l.
- 4-kDa FITC–dextran Papp increased at 10 and 30 min; 40-kDa FITC–dextran Papp unchanged.
- Cevimeline 10 μmol/l (M3 agonist) also dropped TER; 4-DAMP 10 μmol/l blocked carbachol.
- Carbachol reduced claudin-4 protein, not occludin, claudin-1, ZO-1, or E-cadherin. mRNA of claudin-4 unchanged from 5 to 60 min and from 1 to 36 h (supplement cited in text).
- Membrane fraction: claudin-4 decreased from 5 to 60 min. Cytoplasmic claudin-4 transiently increased at 10 min and returned to baseline at 30 and 60 min.
- Phosphorylation target: S195, not S199, S203, or S207.
- Pathway stated by authors: ERK1/2 phosphorylation → β-arrestin2 binding → clathrin-dependent internalization → ubiquitylation / degradation.
- **Not stated in retrieved text:** percent of surface CLDN4 internalized, or an internalization t1/2.

### Ivanov, Nusrat, Parkos, 2004 — Ca2+ depletion, clathrin, storage compartment

- **PMID** 14528017 | **PMC** PMC307538 | **DOI** 10.1091/mbc.e03-05-0319 | *Mol Biol Cell* 15:176–188
- **Access:** `full_text`
- System: T84 intestinal epithelial cells, calcium depletion.
- Junctional proteins internalized include E-cadherin, p120, β-catenin, occludin, JAM-1, claudins 1 and 4, and ZO-1.
- Time window in retrieved text: 30–120 min of depletion, proteins in large subapical ring-like structures.
- Route: caveolae/lipid-raft disruption did not prevent endocytosis; no colocalization with caveolin-1 or dextran (macropinocytosis). Clathrin-pathway inhibitors blocked internalization; junctional proteins colocalized with clathrin and α-adaptin.
- Destination: early endosomes, then organelles that stained with syntaxin-4 but **not** markers of late endosomes, recycling endosomes, lysosomes, or Golgi. Authors call this a unique storage compartment.
- Implication for C1: CLDN4 can leave the junction via clathrin without going to lysosomes in this model.

### Tanaka et al., 2005 — EphA2 phosphorylates CLDN4 Tyr-208

- **PMID** 16236711 | **DOI** 10.1074/jbc.M503786200 | *J Biol Chem*
- **Access:** `abstract_only`
- EphA2 associates with claudin-4 via extracellular domains and phosphorylates the cytoplasmic tail at Tyr-208.
- Phosphorylation attenuates ZO-1 association, decreases integration of claudin-4 into cell–cell contacts, and enhances paracellular permeability.
- Abstract describes delayed tight-junction assembly in MDCK II cells, not an endocytic rate.

## Reviews that compile CLDN4 endocytosis (no new primary numbers)

### Stamatovic et al., 2017 — TJ protein endocytosis review

- **PMC** PMC5479724 (retrieved HTML)
- **Access:** `full_text` of the review
- Lists mAChR-mediated claudin-4 internalization in SMG-C6 (Cong 2015) as a clathrin example.
- Lists poly-L-arginine–induced internalization of occludin, ZO-1, claudin-4, and tricellulin in Caco-2.
- Lists Ca2+-induced uptake of occludin, JAM-1, ZO-1, and claudins-1 and -4 in T84 (Ivanov line of work).
- Mentions ERK1/2 modulation of internalization/degradation of claudin-2, claudin-4, occludin, and ZO-1 (citation in review; that primary was not extracted here).
- No new CLDN4 kinetic constants in the retrieved review text.

### Horowitz et al., 2021 — intestinal TJ endocytosis review

- **PMC** PMC7813749 (retrieved HTML)
- **Access:** `full_text` of the review
- Restates Cong: phosphorylated claudin-4 / β-arrestin 2 → clathrin-dependent internalization.
- States clathrin pits as the main route for claudins generally. Not a CLDN4-specific rate.

## Trafficking neighborhood of CLDN4 (proteomics)

### Fredriksson et al., 2015 — BioID around occludin and claudin-4

- **PMID** 25789658 | **DOI** 10.1371/journal.pone.0117074 | *PLoS ONE* 10:e0117074
- **Access:** `full_text`
- MDCK II cells; biotin ligase fused to Ocln N- or C-terminus or Cldn4 N-terminus; 50 μM biotin, 16 h.
- Inclusion: protein in at least two of three experiments; ≥3-fold enriched vs biotin ligase alone (average normalized PSM/OPN).
- Functional groups near Cldn4 include endocytosis, clathrin-dependent endocytosis, ER/Golgi, and exocytosis/transcytosis.
- Highly recovered trafficking names in the text: YKT6, CLINT1, VAMP2, VAMP5, syntaxin 6.
- Mean coefficient of variation for the five most highly enriched proteins: 0.4 (range 0.1–1).
- TROP-2 is named as a signaling protein enriched only around Cldn4 (see intersection file). **No TROP-2 PSM number was present in the retrieved HTML tables.**

## Interclaudin effects (endocytosis secondary)

### Shashikanth et al., 2022 — claudin-4 interference with other claudins

- **DOI** 10.1038/s41467-022-31587-8 (retrieved HTML)
- **Access:** `full_text`
- Claudin-4 expression triggers endocytic removal of claudins 2 and 15 after first disrupting their strand structures.
- Quoted: “neither dynamin nor clathrin-mediated endocytosis inhibitors prevented mCherry-claudin-4-induced TER increases.”
- This is CLDN4-driven removal of *other* claudins, not a measurement of CLDN4’s own internalization rate.

## What this arm supports for C1

- CLDN4 leaves the plasma membrane / tight junction under defined stimuli (carbachol/mAChR; calcium depletion) by a clathrin-dependent route in the primary papers above.
- Fate is context-dependent: ubiquitin-linked degradation after carbachol (Cong); syntaxin-4 storage compartment, not lysosomes, after Ca2+ depletion (Ivanov).
- Phosphorylation sites stated in primaries: S195 (Cong); Tyr-208 (Tanaka).
- No catalogued primary gives a constitutive CLDN4 internalization t1/2, and none ties CLDN4 endocytosis to a TROP2 ADC.
