!Series_title	"A pan-cancer immune and genomic feature-based classification of tumor microenvironment and resistance to immunotherapy"
!Series_summary	"The tumor microenvironment (TME) profoundly influences responses to immune  checkpoint blockade (ICB) therapies, however characterizing its complexity has been  challenging. While the immune-inflamed, immune-excluded, and immune-desert T  cell immunophenotypes are commonly used to classify the TME, their association  with clinical outcomes to ICBs remains inconsistent. Here we demonstrated that  integrating T cell immunophenotypes and tumor mutational burden (TMB) enables a  more precise stratification of tumors into five distinct subtypes: immune-inflamed  phenotype with high TMB (TMB-H), immune-inflamed phenotype with low TMB  (TMB-L), TMB-H excluded phenotype, TMB-L excluded phenotype, and desert  phenotype. Subsequently, we revealed the underlying mechanisms of tumor resistance  to ICBs of each phenotype within the novel classification and elucidate several  combination treatment approaches aiming at overcoming these inherent resistance  mechanisms. Our study suggested that tailored combination therapy regimens  addressing distinct patterns of immune resistance in different TME subtypes hold  promise for enhancing immunotherapy efficacy"
!Series_overall_design	"The RNA-seq data included in this submission were generated from tumor tissue samples obtained from hospital patients undergoing surgical resection. The samples were collected immediately after surgery and processed into formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) pathological slides. RNA was subsequently extracted from these slides and subjected to RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) to investigate gene expression profiles. The study aims to explore the transcriptomic landscape of tumor tissues, with a focus on identifying differentially expressed genes and potential biomarkers associated with the disease."
